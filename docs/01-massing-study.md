## 0. Working assumptions

The site boundary survey is not yet available. All geometry below is
built on a **working rectangle of 56 × 52 m of buildable area**
(2,912 m² outline) derived from the 4,621.80 m² plot with indicative
setbacks. Every plate figure moves when the survey lands. The road
reservation to the Ambo Road is the single largest unknown.

Orientation convention used throughout: the plot is a corner site with
roads to the **north-east** and **south-east**. Its four edges therefore
face NE, SE, SW and NW — all at roughly 45° to the cardinal points.

## 1. Findings from the primary source

Six points where the governing standard, read directly, changes or
confirms the brief.

### 1.1 Art. 8.2(6) — departmental rooms are 96 m² in total, not each

The Afaan Oromo reads *"Kutaalee muummeewwanii 4 **bal'inni waliigalaa
isaanii** kaaree meetira 96 (8m x 12m) ta'ee"* — "four departmental
rooms, **their combined area** 96 m²."

The parallel provision for secondary, Art. 8.3(5), removes the doubt:
*"Kutaa muummeewwanii 4 … bal'inni isaa kaaree meetira 120 ta'ee
kutaalee arfanuu qofa qofaatti … bal'inni isaanii karee meetira 30 (5m
x 6m)"* — total 120 m², **each** 30 m². The Bureau's own drafting
pattern is total-then-each, and it is content with 30 m² departmental
rooms at secondary level.

The brief adopts 96 m² each (384 m² total) and flags the ambiguity.
On this reading the correct figure is **96 m² total, 24 m² each** —
a reduction of 288 m² net, roughly 400 m² GFA. This should still be
confirmed with the Bureau, but the reading now runs in our favour.

### 1.2 Art. 15 — narrow the consolidation ask

The brief seeks blanket consolidation and prices refusal at 1,809 m²
net. Art. 15 does not require full duplication; it requires that
co-located schools *"bulchiinsa ofii isaanii akka qabaatan"* — maintain
their own administration.

A narrower and far more grantable position: **accept duplicated
directors' offices, argue that staff rooms, food stores, dining,
sanitary and guard accommodation are shared compound facilities.**

| Position | Added net area |
|---|---|
| Full duplication (4 KG + 2 primary) | 1,809 m² |
| Directors' offices only (3 × 20 + 1 × 24) | **84 m²** |

That is a swing of roughly 2,330 m² GFA — most of a storey — and it
reduces the project's regulatory dependency to the Art. 6 compound-area
waiver alone. Art. 11(4), read carefully, sets *minimum* enrolments for
opening a new school (KG 60, primary 200, secondary 160), not caps,
which strengthens the case that Art. 11(1)–(3) are standards rather
than prohibitions.

### 1.3 The sanitation problem is unaddressed

Arts. 8.2(13) and 8.3(17) specify *boolii* — **pits** — at one per 25
students, 1.08 m² (0.9 × 1.2 m) each. Pit latrines cannot be stacked
above ground. On a G+6 building this necessarily becomes full
waterborne sanitation:

- ~144 WCs (KG 24, primary 80, secondary 40)
- 25–40 m³/day of wastewater
- no room on 4,621 m² for a conventional septic tank and soakaway

The resolution is an on-site packaged treatment plant (DEWATS baffled
reactor plus planted gravel filter, or MBBR) with treated effluent
irrigating the Art. 7(4) agricultural terrace and flushing WCs. This
has a land, cost and basement implication **now**, at massing stage,
not at services stage. It also happens to be the strongest
sustainability argument the scheme has, and it is a necessity rather
than a gesture.

### 1.4 Water storage is a structural load, not a note

Art. 5(3) makes a serviced site a precondition. At 3,600 students plus
staff with waterborne sanitation, two days' storage is 70–100 m³ — up
to **1,000 kN** on the roof, concentrated. Split it: a large ground or
basement suction tank, a small (20–25 m³) roof service tank, pumped.
The brief's stack lists "plant, water storage" at roof without
quantifying it.

### 1.5 The five *Golee* argue for cluster planning

Art. 17(1) requires each KG classroom to be divided into **five learning
corners** — language, mathematics, science, social/cultural, and art and
handcraft (elaborated at Arts. 18.1(1)–(5)). Thirty children plus five
activity corners in 63 m² is 2.1 m² per child. The corners will not fit
as furniture inside the room.

They fit as a **shared commons**: five classrooms opening onto one
*Golee* commons that holds the five corners at proper size. Four such
clusters give 20 classrooms — and four clusters is exactly the four KG
units Art. 11(1) would have us register. The registration structure and
the pedagogical structure become the same thing, expressed
architecturally rather than waived.

The same logic gives primary ten clusters of four and secondary five
clusters of five. This is also how Principle 5 ("circulation is
programme") gets delivered without simply widening corridors.

### 1.6 Every large room fits an 18–20 m wing

Checked against the schedule:

| Room | Dimension | Deepest side |
|---|---|---|
| Multipurpose hall, 8.3(9) | 16.25 × 26.20 | 16.25 |
| Secondary library, 8.3(10) | 16 × 17 | 16 |
| Science laboratory, 8.3(8) | 22 × 9 | 9 |
| Workshop, 8.3(14) | 24 × 8 | 8 |
| Primary library, 8.2(4) | 12 × 15 | 12 |

Nothing in the schedule needs more than 16.25 m of depth. A ring or
wing of 18–20 m accommodates the entire programme, which is what makes
a courtyard typology viable here at all.

## 2. The reframe: outdoor area is the scarce resource

The brief's headline metric is 1.28 m² of *land* per student. The
metric that should drive the massing is **usable outdoor area per
student**, because that is what the vertical typology destroys and must
then manufacture back.

- Land per student: 1.28 m²
- Open ground remaining after a 63% footprint: ~1,700 m² → **0.47 m²
  per student**
- With the terracing proposed below: ~4,600 m² → **1.28 m² per
  student**

Terracing does not add outdoor space to a generous site. It recovers
the ground the building consumed, almost exactly once over. Every
massing option below is therefore judged first on **how much
contiguous, usable, shaded deck it generates**, and only then on
elegance.

## 3. The tyranny of 32 × 19

A FIBA court plus run-off is 32 × 19 m, 608 m². The 19 m clear
dimension is the hardest geometric constraint in the project and it
governs the massing more than anything else, because a classroom wing
is 17–18 m deep (7 + corridor + 7). **A court will not sit on the roof
of a classroom wing.** It needs either:

1. the roof of a deeper, single-storey volume (the hall and workshop),
2. the top of the building, where the plate is unobstructed, or
3. a setback of 19 m in one move, which costs 600–1,200 m² of plate.

This single fact eliminates two of the four massing families below.

### 3.1 Recommendation — invert the brief's sport/greenery stacking

The brief puts the 900 m² sports deck low, at Level 1, so that primary
students can reach it, and distributes greenery and agriculture upward.
I would invert this:

| | Brief | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Roof (L6) | Plant, running loop | **Full court + running loop** |
| Upper decks | Greenery | Secondary study terraces |
| L1–L3 decks | Sports court at L1 | **Soft play, outdoor teaching, agriculture** |

Reasoning:

- Formal court sport is a **timetabled, supervised, secondary-weighted**
  activity. The secondary school sits at L5–L6. Putting the court on the
  roof puts it *closer* to its main users, not further.
- Young children need **frequent, brief, adjacent** outdoor access —
  not one large hard court five floors below their classroom. KG and
  lower primary want a 250–300 m² soft deck they can spill onto in four
  minutes, which is what the low terraces should be.
- The roof plate (~1,300 m²) takes the court *and* a 170 m perimeter
  running loop with no cost to any classroom floor. At Level 1 the same
  court costs 900 m² of plate, which must then be recovered by building
  higher or wider.
- Impact noise stops being a problem. Nothing sits beneath the roof
  court but plant and the top-floor ceiling void.

The cost is that the roof needs the 4–6 m ball-catch enclosure the brief
already identifies as the defining skyline element, plus lift access for
equipment. Both are wanted anyway.

## 4. Four massing families, tested

Common inputs: 56 × 52 m buildable rectangle, G+6, ground floor 4.5 m,
upper floors 3.6 m, target GFA 13,700–14,700 m².

### A. Concentric ziggurat — *fails on arithmetic*

Plates inset equally on all four sides, as the brief's Figure 6
implies.

| Step per side | GFA | Terrace width |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 m | 12,425 m² | 1.5 m — unusable |
| 1.0 m | 14,112 m² | 1.0 m — unusable |

Concentric stepping loses area from all four sides at once, so it either
runs 1,300 m² short of the programme or produces terraces one metre
wide. The largest contiguous deck it can generate below roof level is
about 80 m². It also cannot host the court anywhere but the roof, and it
requires seven different floor plates with seven different formwork
sets. **Discard.**

### B. Single-sided cascade — *converges on C*

Full-height slab on the NE (road) edge, stepping down toward the SW.
With 6 m steps every two floors the GFA lands at ~14,000 m² with three
usable 6 × 56 m terraces. But the ground plate is 46 m deep, and a 46 m
deep floor cannot satisfy the brief's own hard constraint of no desk
more than 7.5 m from a window without an internal void. Once the void is
inserted, this is Option C with one side removed. The 6 m terraces also
still cannot take the court.

### C. Eroding courtyard — *recommended*

A ring around a central void, from which single large notches are
removed as the building rises, spiralling around the four faces.

- Outline 56 × 52 m; courtyard 32 × 14 m (448 m²)
- NE wing 18 m deep (7 + 4 + 7) — classrooms facing the road and court
- SW wing 20 m deep (7 + 6 + 7) — the deep "learning street" on the hot
  side, doubling as the veranda buffer
- End wings 12–14 m — cores, sanitary, specialist rooms

Indicative stack:

| Level | Plate | Deck | Programme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roof | — | 1,320 | Court 32 × 19, 170 m running loop, plant |
| L6 | 1,316 | 168 | Secondary, departmental rooms |
| L5 | 1,484 | 280 | Secondary, ICT, study terrace |
| L4 | 1,764 | 252 | Primary upper, science kit rooms |
| L3 | 2,016 | 168 | Primary, agricultural terrace |
| L2 | 2,184 | — | Primary lower, SEN centre, day care |
| L1 | 2,184 | 280 | Kindergarten, four *Golee* clusters |
| Ground | 2,464 | — | Hall, libraries, workshop, labs, dining, three entrances |
| **Total** | **13,412** | **2,468** | plus 448 m² courtyard at grade |

Why it works:

- **The court sets the wing depth, and the wing depth buys the learning
  street.** Needing 19 m clear pushes the SW wing to 20 m, which yields
  a 6 m wide daylit circulation spine — Principle 5 delivered by
  geometry rather than by generosity.
- Notches are **few and large** (168–280 m²) instead of many and thin.
  A 14 × 14 m deck is an outdoor classroom for fifty; a 1.5 m ledge is
  nothing. Each notch also self-shades the facade two floors below it.
- The spiral of notches gives the silhouette its identity without exotic
  geometry, and every column still runs uninterrupted to foundation.
- No point on any floor is more than 9 m from either the outer facade or
  the courtyard. Every classroom is double-aspect.
- Ring depth 18–20 m accepts every large room in the schedule (§1.6).

**The courtyard — *Gaaddisa*.** Art. 2(12) of the standard defines
*Gaaddisa Dubbisaa* as a shaded outdoor reading place under trees with
good air. The concept name should come from the regulation rather than
be imposed on it. The void is roofed at high level with an open-sided
louvred canopy: it becomes a genuine solar chimney driving stack
ventilation, the flag-ceremony space of Art. 7(2) usable during the
kiremt rains, a ~450 m² rainwater catchment, and the one space every
student passes through daily with every corridor balcony overlooking it
(Principle 8, passive supervision).

### D. Comb of three bars — *does not fit*

Three 18 m bars with 8 m gaps need 70 m in one direction; the buildable
rectangle offers 56. The comb degenerates to two bars, which is Option C
with both ends open. It also carries roughly 50% more external wall per
square metre of floor than the courtyard ring — on Ethiopian
reinforced-concrete cost structure, in the order of 6–8% on total build
cost. **Discard on this site.**

### Comparison

| | A ziggurat | B cascade | C courtyard | D comb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFA achieved | 12,400–14,100 | ~14,000 | ~13,400 | ~13,600 |
| Largest deck below roof | 80 m² | 336 m² | **280 m²** | 300 m² |

## 5. Orientation — a respectful disagreement

The brief identifies rotating the dominant axis toward east–west as
"the single most valuable move available to the design." At 9°N I would
weight this differently, and would test rather than assume it.

The site's edges run at ~45° to the cardinals. Rotating a building to
true east–west inside a 45°-skewed corner plot leaves triangular
residual land on all four sides — at 1.28 m² per student, the least
affordable geometry available. Meanwhile the solar penalty of the
site's natural grain is smaller than temperate intuition suggests,
because at this latitude the noon sun sits at 65–85° and the governing
problem is the low east and west sun at 10–30°, which no plan rotation
solves. Only section does.

The proposal is therefore: **hold the plan to the site grain; solve
solar in section.**

- Classrooms face NE and SE (the better orientations) and the courtyard.
- The 20 m SW wing puts its 6 m learning street, stairs and veranda on
  the hot afternoon face — a thermal buffer, not a habitable room.
- Perforated hollow-block screens, vertical on the west-facing faces
  and horizontal on the north and south, per Art. 9.1's silence on
  screening.
- The road faces (NE, SE) get the taut, controlled elevation and take
  the dust and noise; the quiet residential faces (SW, NW) get the
  cascading planted notches and the view to Wechecha.

An orientation study comparing the site-grain scheme against a rotated
scheme is a Phase 1 deliverable regardless. I would simply not presume
the answer.

## 6. Structural grid

Three constraints resolve into one grid.

- Primary/secondary classroom 56 m², Art. 8.2(1)/8.3(1)
- KG classroom 63 m², Art. 8.1(1)
- 32 × 19 m clear on the sports deck

**Grid: 8.0 m bays longitudinally; 7.0 | 4.0 | 7.0 transverse in the NE
wing, 7.0 | 6.0 | 7.0 in the SW wing.**

- Classroom = 8.0 × 7.0 = 56.0 m² exactly. ✓
- KG classroom = 8.0 × 7.875 = 63.0 m² exactly — the same 8 m grid with
  the facade pushed out 0.875 m. Since the KG band is the widest part of
  a mass that only diminishes upward, that bulge is structurally free
  and becomes the podium edge. The guideline regulates area, not the
  7 × 9 parenthetical. ✓
- Seven 8 m bays = 56 m, the full wing length. Fourteen classrooms per
  full floor; 85 classrooms across six upper levels. ✓
- Columns land on partition lines and in the circulation zone, never
  mid-room — critical at Level 1 where 900–1,200 mm columns arrive in
  kindergarten accommodation that has no spare area.
- Lateral stability from shear walls to the courtyard cores, not frame
  action, per the brief.

## 7. What I need before Massing 02

1. **Topographic survey and boundary dimensions**, plus the Ambo Road
   reservation. Everything in Section 4 is parametric until this lands.
2. **A decision on the sport inversion** (§3.1). It unlocks the
   courtyard; without it the low-level court consumes the plate that
   makes the courtyard possible.
3. **Is a basement viable?** The brief never raises one. A single
   basement of ~2,400 m² would absorb the water tanks, the treatment
   plant, plant rooms, bulk stores, kitchen support and staff parking,
   releasing 500–800 m² of ground floor to teaching and play. At this
   density it may be the cheapest area on the site. It needs the
   geotechnical report and the water table before it can be priced.
4. **The permitted site coverage and setbacks** under the local plan.
   The scheme sits at 63% outline coverage; if the ceiling is 50% the
   whole study restarts.
5. **Budget envelope**, total and per m², to calibrate the facade
   ambition against the G+6 economic ceiling the brief identifies.
