### 1.3 The finding that matters

**The site is very nearly cardinally aligned.** The east boundary runs
within 1.75° of true north–south; the north boundary within 6.3° of true
east–west.

This contradicts the premise in Design Brief 01 §10.2 — *"The site
geometry does not naturally yield an east–west axis"* — and equally
contradicts Massing Study 01 §6, which argued for holding the site grain
on the assumption that the grain was 45° off. Both were working from the
verbal description of a corner plot "bounded by roads on the north-east
and south-east." The survey says otherwise.

The consequence is favourable and removes an argument rather than
creating one: **a building aligned to the site boundaries is also
aligned east–west.** There is no trade to make. Long north and south
elevations, shaded by simple 1.2 m horizontal overhangs, come free.

### 1.4 The buildable envelope is smaller than the extent suggests

The north and south boundaries are diagonal, so the plot tapers to a
wedge at both ends. The largest true-cardinal rectangle that fits inside
the polygon is:

> **51 m east–west × 68 m north–south = 3,468 m²**
> (south-west corner at E 464 058.9, N 996 758.5)

The usable north–south depth for cardinally aligned building is
therefore about **65–68 m, not 84.5 m**. The residual ~1,150 m² lies in
a western and southern wedge — good for landscape, drop-off, the
agricultural plot and the perimeter greenery, but not for orthogonal
building.

This single figure governs the massing. It is why three parallel
classroom bars plus a 19 m sports slot will not fit, and why the scheme
resolves as two bars and a link. See Massing Study R2 §3.

## 2. Geotechnical report — review

### 2.1 The boreholes are not on this site

| | Easting | Northing |
|---|---|---|
| Site polygon | 464 041.9 – 464 112.5 | **996 749.5 – 996 833.9** |
| Boreholes BH-1 to BH-6 | 464 073 – 464 099 | **996 665 – 996 713** |

The eastings overlap. The northings do not. Every borehole lies south of
the site's southern boundary:

| BH | Northing | Distance south of boundary |
|---|---|---|
| BH-1 | 996 713 | 36 m |
| BH-2 | 996 705 | 44 m |
| BH-3 | 996 697 | 52 m |
| BH-4 | 996 691 | 58 m |
| BH-5 | 996 674 | 75 m |
| BH-6 | 996 665 | 84 m |

Supporting discrepancies: the report is addressed to **Three M JY
Trading Plc**, not Boriyad Youth Academy, and locates the works in
*"Sheger city, Melka Nano Sub city around Amfomeda"* rather than Ashewa
Meda.

Three readings are possible: the report belongs to the adjacent parcel;
the hand-held GPS positions carry a datum error (the report states UTM
Adindan, and an Adindan/WGS84 mismatch produces offsets of this order in
Ethiopia); or the survey and the report use different datums. A
hand-held GPS *precision* error is ±5–10 m and cannot account for 36–84 m.

**Position: the report cannot be used for foundation design on this
site.** It is useful as regional indicative data — the geology is
laterally consistent in this part of the plain — but boreholes must be
drilled inside the polygon. Given the rockhead variability below, a
minimum of four holes to 15 m within the footprint.

### 2.2 If read as indicative — the ground

| Layer | Description | Thickness |
|---|---|---|
| A | Soft to medium stiff, dark grey, high plastic silty CLAY — **black cotton** | 2.0–3.0 m |
| B | Stiff to very stiff, reddish brown, high plastic silty CLAY with sand and gravel | to 3.5–15.0 m |
| C | Weak to moderately weak, highly weathered, closely jointed, highly fractured vesicular BASALT | to end of holes |

**Rockhead is highly variable: 3.5 m (BH-5, BH-6) to 15.0 m (BH-2)**,
over a spread of about 50 m, rising toward the south. The report's
conclusion that *"uniform soil layers are identified"* is not supported
by its own data.

**No groundwater was encountered in any hole to 10–15 m.** Only surface
water, attributed to the rainy season and a "swampy area". This is the
single most basement-friendly fact in the report — no dewatering, no
tanking against head, no uplift check.

### 2.3 The expansive soil is severe

Free swell (FW) and plasticity from Table 6:

| BH | Depth | LL | PI | USCS | FW | Swell potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BH-4 | 1.50–2.00 | 105 | **63** | CH | **130** | Very high |
| BH-2 | 2.50–3.00 | 82 | **45** | CH | **120** | Very high |
| BH-3 | 2.50–3.00 | 66 | 31 | CH | 30 | Moderate |
| BH-5 | 2.50–3.00 | 66 | 36 | CH | 30 | Moderate |
| BH-1 | 3.00–3.50 | 57 | 32 | CH | 20 | Moderate |
| BH-2 | 4.50–5.00 | 58 | 31 | CH | 20 | Moderate |
| BH-1 | 5.50–6.00 | 50 | 26 | CH | 10 | Low |

A plasticity index of 63 with a liquid limit of 105 is extreme. Two of
the twelve samples fall in the very high swell class, and the BH-2
sample sits at **2.50–3.00 m — at the report's own recommended founding
depth of 3.00 m.**

**This is the most consequential technical point in the report, and the
report does not address it.** Founding at 3.00 m places footings within
or immediately above very high swell material at two of six locations.
The active zone in Addis Ababa black cotton is typically 2.5–4.0 m.

### 2.4 Bearing capacity — the recommendation is not the worst case

Tables 10 and 11 adopt **BH-3** as the design case for both G+4 and G+6,
giving 358–385 kPa at 3.00 m depth. BH-2 is materially weaker:

| B (m) | Table 10/11 recommendation (BH-3) | BH-2, Table 8, D = 3.0 m | Overestimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 385 kPa | 328 kPa | 17% |
| 2.5 | 367 kPa | 285 kPa | 29% |
| 3.0 | 364 kPa | 275 kPa | **32%** |
| 4.0 | 360 kPa | 247 kPa | **46%** |

The report also gives an identical recommendation for G+4 and G+6, which
cannot be right for two different load cases on variable ground.

**Position: pending site-specific boreholes, carry 275 kPa allowable at
3.00 m depth.** Even at that figure the ground is more than adequate —
see §3.1.

### 2.5 Seismic

Zone 3, PGA 0.10 g, per ES EN 1998:2015. The report assigns importance
factor **I = 1.0**. Under ES EN 1998-1 a school is Importance Class III,
γ<sub>I</sub> = 1.2. The design seismic action is therefore **0.12 g**,
not 0.10 g. Flag to the structural engineer so the report's figure is
not carried forward unadjusted.

Moderate seismicity at G+6 places a premium on **plan regularity**: no
torsional irregularity, no re-entrant corners, shear-wall centroid close
to the mass centroid. This is a direct argument against the spiralling
set-backs proposed in Massing Study 01 §5, and in favour of separate
regular rectangular volumes with seismic movement joints. See R2 §4.

## 3. What this means for the design

### 3.1 The bearing capacity is not the constraint; the swell is

Estimated column load, G+6, 8.0 × 7.0 m grid, school loading
(≈12 kPa per level including live):

> 12 kPa × 7 levels × 56 m² ≈ **4,700 kN** service

At 275 kPa allowable that is a 4.15 m square pad; at 364 kPa, 3.6 m.
Either is comfortable — the ground is strong. But a 4.15 m pad on a
7.0 m grid, and particularly across the 4.0 m corridor bay, **will clash
with its neighbour.** Combined footings or a strip under the corridor
line are required, which is itself an argument for a raft wherever the
basement occurs.

### 3.2 The basement is a geotechnical mitigation that partly pays for itself

This is the strongest single finding of the review, and it validates the
client's decision.

- The design must reach below the black cotton regardless. The report
  founds at 3.00 m for exactly that reason.
- A basement slab at ~4.0 m below existing ground takes the whole
  structure clear of the active zone, **removes** the expansive material
  rather than founding within it, and eliminates the heave risk to the
  ground floor.
- No groundwater to 15 m means no dewatering, no tanking against
  hydrostatic head, no uplift design.
- The basement roof slab is a suspended ground floor by definition —
  which is what expansive soil demands anyway, and which would otherwise
  be a cost with no return.

**The marginal excavation for a basement is roughly 1.0 m beyond the
3.0 m already required.** That makes it far cheaper here than the usual
1.3–1.6× ground-floor rate, and it buys accommodation that would
otherwise consume ground floor the site cannot spare.

Caution: rockhead at 3.5 m in the southern holes. If that trend extends
under the site, a 4.0 m basement excavates fractured basalt — rippable
with a hydraulic breaker, not blasting, but a real cost and programme
item. The rockhead trend deepens northward (15 m at BH-2, the
northernmost hole), so the site may sit over deep clay. **Site-specific
boreholes will decide where the basement goes.** Locate it where
rockhead is deepest.

### 3.3 Where there is no basement

Client instruction is one basement, not the full footprint. Everywhere
else:

- Suspended ground floor slab on a void former or granular blanket —
  never ground-bearing.
- Footings to 3.5–4.0 m, or excavate and replace layer A with compacted
  granular fill.
- Wide perimeter apron with positive fall; the site is flat, swampy in
  the rains, and the clay is impermeable — **soakaways will not work.**
  Stormwater must be attenuated, harvested, or piped out.
- **No high-water-demand trees near the buildings.** Eucalyptus in
  particular must be kept beyond about 1.5 times mature height of any
  footing. This directly conflicts with the guideline's *Gaaddisa
  Dubbisaa* — a reading place under trees — and with the Art. 7(5)
  greenery. Both must go in the court and the western wedge, at
  distance, or in raised planters.

### 3.4 Rainwater harvesting becomes a serious proposition

Roof area ≈ 2,414 m², annual rainfall 1,089 mm, run-off coefficient 0.8:

> ≈ **2,100 m³ per year**

Against an estimated demand of 30 m³/day × ~200 school days = 6,000 m³,
harvesting can supply roughly a third of annual consumption. With a
cistern in the basement and the treated-effluent scheme from Massing
Study 01 §1.3, the two together substantially decouple the school from
mains supply — on a site where Art. 5(3) makes servicing a precondition.
