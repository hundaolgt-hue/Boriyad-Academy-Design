## 2. Two premises overturned

### 2.1 The site is cardinally aligned

The east boundary runs 1.75° off true north–south; the north boundary
6.31° off true east–west. Design Brief 01 §10.2 assumed the geometry
"does not naturally yield an east–west axis", and Massing Study 01 §6
argued for holding a grain believed to be 45° off cardinal.

Both were wrong, and the error was mine to the extent that I argued a
position from the brief's description rather than waiting for the
survey. **Building parallel to the boundaries now delivers the east–west
axis for free.** Every classroom can face north or south, shaded by a
1.2 m horizontal overhang. This is the largest passive-design win in the
project and it costs nothing.

### 2.2 The usable depth is 68 m, not 84.5 m

The north and south boundaries are diagonal, so the plot wedges at both
ends. The largest true-cardinal rectangle inside the polygon is **51 m
east–west × 68 m north–south**. The residual ~1,150 m² is a western and
southern wedge — landscape, drop-off, agriculture, not building.

This kills the three-bar comb that the corrected orientation would
otherwise have favoured:

> three 17 m bars = 51 m, leaving 17 m for two slots — 8.5 m each. A
> 19 m sports slot forces the other to zero.

So the arithmetic returns two bars and a link.

## 3. Where the court goes — the answer moved past the inversion

Massing Study 01 §3.1 proposed lifting the court from Level 1 to the
roof. With the real geometry there is a better answer: **the court comes
all the way down to grade, into the open court between the bars.**

At 52% coverage the site holds a 41 × 31 m open court — enough for the
32 × 19 m FIBA rectangle with run-off and an edge. At grade the court
costs nothing:

| | L1 deck (Brief 01) | Roof (Study 01) | **Grade (R2)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plate consumed | 900 m² | none | none |
| Structure | 5.0 kN/m² deck | 5.0 kN/m² roof | none |
| Impact isolation | required | not required | not required |
| Ball-catch enclosure | 4–6 m, mid-height | 4–6 m on the skyline | perimeter fence |
| Waterproofing | inverted roof under sports surface | as L1 | none |
| Supervision | deck-level only | remote | overlooked from every classroom |

It also resolves Art. 7 almost literally. The guideline requires the
compound divided into five zones — buildings, flag ceremony, sports
field, agricultural plot, greenery. Four of the five now sit in one
south-facing court at grade, visible from the road and from every
classroom above.

**On the flag ceremony (client point 4).** The court is 1,288 m². At
0.5 m² per student in ranks that is 2,576 — the whole primary school at
once, or any one school comfortably, with staggered assembly across the
three (which the brief already assumes for stair and lift loading, and
which Art. 12 permits since full-day operation is retained).

### 4.3 Why this is the cost-effective answer

Massing Study 01 §4 discarded the multi-volume option on facade area per
square metre of floor (0.64 against 0.46). That comparison was too
narrow. Against the extra envelope:

- **Three separate regular rectangular volumes with seismic movement
  joints.** In Zone 3 at 0.12 g this is the ideal configuration — no
  torsional irregularity, no re-entrant corners, shear-wall centroid on
  the mass centroid in each volume. The eroding courtyard of Study 01,
  with its spiralling set-backs, was plan-irregular at every level and
  would have carried a real seismic penalty. That was an error of
  architecture running ahead of engineering.
- **Repetitive formwork.** Three plain rectangular frames on one 8.0 m
  grid, no transfer, no long spans, no post-tensioning. The single
  largest cost lever available in Ethiopian reinforced-concrete
  construction.
- **Everything the roof court would have cost is deleted** — the
  5.0 kN/m² deck, the impact isolation, the inverted roof under a sports
  surface, the elevated ball-catch enclosure.
- **Phased handover becomes straightforward.** Bar 1, holding the
  kindergarten, is a four-storey building that can be completed and
  occupied while the taller volumes proceed. Brief 01 §9.3 wanted this
  and judged it difficult on a single-core building. Here it falls out
  of the massing.

The honest cost against: three volumes mean three sets of cores, stairs
and sanitary stacks. But Principle 3 — three schools, three entrances,
three cores — required that anyway.

### 4.4 What carries forward unchanged

- **8.0 m grid**; classroom 8.0 × 7.0 = 56.00 m²; KG 8.0 × 7.875 =
  63.00 m². Bars 17 m deep (7 + 3 + 7).
- **KG in clusters of five around a shared *Golee* commons** — four
  clusters, matching the four registrable KG units.
- **Sanitary stacks aligned vertically**, ~144 WCs, waterborne, with the
  packaged treatment plant in the basement and treated effluent
  irrigating the agricultural plot.
- **Deep verandas and screens on the west-facing ends**, which are now
  the short ends of the bars rather than a long elevation — a further
  gain from the corrected orientation.

### 4.5 Open points

1. **Which boundary is the Ambo Road?** It determines the three
   entrances, the drop-off, and which face takes the acoustic and dust
   screening. The scheme assumes the eastern boundary is the primary
   road — hence the link placed there as a shield, with the kindergarten
   at the far south-west, quietest corner. Confirm against the road
   hierarchy.
2. **Road reservation setback** on each frontage. Still not quantified;
   the scheme carries 2–3 m and has 11 percentage points of coverage in
   hand.
3. **Site-specific boreholes** — four to 15 m inside the polygon, to fix
   the basement location and the founding depth. See Ground and Survey
   Review §2.6.
4. Confirm whether "move the court" was also intended to move the
   *courtyard*. Under R2 both move together: the enclosed courtyard of
   Study 01 becomes the open court at grade, so the two readings
   converge.
