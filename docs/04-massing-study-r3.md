# Massing Study R3 — three separate schools

Supersedes Massing Study R2 §4 (Scheme E). R2 §§1–3 — the client
decisions, the survey findings and the court-at-grade argument — stand.

New requirement: **the three sections must be separated and must have
different entrances.**

---

## 1. Why Scheme E failed the requirement

R2 assigned the kindergarten and primary lower school to the same
south bar, and split the primary school across that bar and the eastern
link. Under the new instruction that is not viable: one school, one
volume, one entrance.

The requirement is not cosmetic. It forces the footprint, because each
school must now be sized independently rather than sharing plates.

| School | Net area | Gross at 1.40 | Footprint needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kindergarten, 20 classrooms | 1,617 m² | 2,264 m² | 1,132 m² at 2 levels |
| Primary, 40 classrooms | 4,343 m² * | 6,080 m² | 869 m² at 7 levels |
| Secondary, 25 classrooms | 3,912 m² | 5,477 m² | 783 m² at 7 levels |
| **Total** | **9,872 m²** | **13,821 m²** | **2,784 m² · 60.3%** |

\* after the Art. 8.2(6) correction of −288 m² (Study 01 §1.1).

Coverage is fine — 60.3% against a 63% ceiling. **The problem is not
area, it is depth.**

---

## 2. The constraint that now governs

The usable cardinal depth of the plot is 68 m (Ground and Survey Review
§1.4). A classroom bar is 17–19 m. A court that takes the 32 × 19 m
FIBA rectangle needs 21 m of depth at minimum.

> three bars + one court = 17 + 17 + 17 + 21 = **72 m** against 68 m
> available, before a single setback or separation joint.

Add 2 m joints and 2–3 m setbacks and the shortfall is roughly 12 m.
**Three parallel east–west bars, a grade-level full court, and 63%
coverage cannot all coexist on this site.** One of the four has to give.

| | What it costs |
|---|---|
| **F1 · Kindergarten at G+2** | Departs from our own Principle 1 (KG no higher than Level 1). No regulation is breached — the guideline sets no height limit for kindergarten. Frees ~375 m² of footprint. |
| **F2 · Court returns to a roof** | Reinstates the 5.0 kN/m² deck, the waterproofing under a sports surface and the elevated ball-catch enclosure that R2 deleted. Frees ~1,100 m² at grade. |
| **F3 · Full court omitted** | Art. 7(3) requires a sports field, not a FIBA rectangle — that dimension was our own standard. Cross-courts only, competitive play off-site. |
| **F4 · Two schools stacked in one volume** | Separate lobbies and cores, no internal connection. Satisfies "different entrances" but not "separated". |

**Recommendation: F1.** It is the smallest concession and the only one
that gives something back. A three-storey kindergarten with the youngest
children at ground, a terrace on every floor, an external ramp and two
direct stairs to grade is ordinary practice in dense cities and better
for the children than a two-storey slab that swallows their garden. F4
is excluded by the instruction; F2 and F3 both give up something the
client has already valued.

entrance, own cores, own administration, and its own gate onto a shared
court.

### 3.2 The three entrances

| School | Gate | Arrival | Separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kindergarten | South-west | Own drop-off loop in the southern wedge, direct into a walled garden. Furthest point on the site from the main road. | — |
| Secondary | East road, south end | Forecourt in the south-east wedge; the basement service ramp shares it, away from the court. | 55 m from the primary gate |
| Primary | East road, north end | Forecourt at the north-east corner. | 55 m from the secondary gate |

Each school reaches the shared court through its own controlled gate, so
the court can be timetabled by school without any mixing of age groups
in circulation.

### 3.3 The court

Bounded by the kindergarten to the south, the secondary to the east, the
primary to the north, and open to the western landscape wedge.

| | |
|---|---|
| Area | ~1,176 m² |
| Clear dimensions | 40 m average × 29 m |
| Sports | 32 × 19 m FIBA rectangle with run-off |
| Flag ceremony | 2,940 at 0.4 m² per student — the full primary school in one assembly |

**The tallest volume sits on the north side of the court, which at 9° N
is exactly right.** The sun tracks south of zenith for eight and a half
months of the year, so a seven-storey building to the north casts its
shadow away from the court, not across it. The secondary volume on the
east shades the court in early morning only. The kindergarten on the
south is three storeys and casts a short shadow during the kiremt rains,
when the court is least used.

### 3.4 Orientation — the one compromise

The kindergarten and the primary run true east–west, so every classroom
in both faces north or south and needs only a 1.2 m horizontal overhang.
The secondary runs north–south along the road, and its long faces
therefore look east and west.

This falls on the secondary deliberately:

- It has the fewest classrooms — 25 against the primary's 40.
- It carries the area-hungry specialist rooms — three 22 × 9 m
  laboratories, the 24 × 8 m workshop, the 425.75 m² hall, the 272 m²
  library — which are indifferent to orientation and take the eastern,
  road-facing side where they also buffer traffic noise.
- Its classrooms face west over the court, behind a 3 m veranda with
  vertical fins. Horizontal shading is useless at 10–30° solar
  altitude; vertical fins are the correct device, and this veranda is
  where Brief 01 §4.3 always intended the building's character to sit.
  It overlooks the sports and the assembly — the best room in the school.
- Oldest students are the ones best able to use a shaded outdoor
  circulation space rather than a sealed corridor.

Each school now owns its own roof terrace outright, which the shared-mass

### 3.6 Basement

**810 m² under the secondary volume** — one basement, not the full
footprint, per the client decision. Vehicle ramp from the south-east
forecourt, so service traffic never crosses the court or the
kindergarten zone. Contents: bulk water 80 m³, rainwater cistern,
packaged treatment plant, pumps, generator and MV switchgear, bulk
stores, kitchen support.

Final location to be confirmed against the new boreholes — put it where
rockhead is deepest (Ground and Survey Review §3.2).

### 3.7 What separation buys, and what it costs

**Buys:**
- Principle 3 delivered literally — three schools, three entrances,
  three cores, staggered arrival, sharing one court.
- Art. 15 satisfied without argument. Each school demonstrably maintains
  its own administration in its own building, which narrows the
  regulatory ask to the Art. 6 compound-area waiver alone.
- Three independent regular rectangular structures with movement joints
  between them. At 0.12 g this is the ideal seismic configuration.
- Phasing becomes trivial. The kindergarten is a three-storey building
  that can be tendered, built and occupied on its own.
- Evacuation simplifies — three separate populations, three separate
  egress systems, no shared stair carrying 3,600 people.

**Costs:**
- Three sets of cores, lifts, stacks and plant, rather than shared ones.
- More external envelope per square metre of floor.
- No shared hall or library across the schools. Under the guideline this
  is correct in any case — Art. 8.2(4) schedules primary libraries
  separately from the Art. 8.3(10) secondary library, and the Art. 8.3(9)
  multipurpose hall belongs to the secondary school. Nothing in the
  schedule actually needed to be shared.

That last point is worth noting: Brief 01 Principle 3 assumed the three
schools would share "one hall, one library spine and one sports deck."
Read against the schedule, only the sports provision is genuinely shared
— and that is the court, which all three now front.

## 4. Carried forward

- 8.0 m grid. Classroom 8.0 × 7.0 = 56.00 m²; kindergarten 8.0 × 7.875
  = 63.00 m². Bars 17–18 m deep, 7 + 3 + 7.
- Kindergarten in clusters of five around a shared *Golee* commons —
  four clusters over three levels, matching the four registrable units.
- Vertically aligned sanitary stacks, ~144 WCs, waterborne, treatment
  plant in the basement, treated effluent irrigating the agricultural
  plot in the court.
- No high-water-demand trees within ~1.5 × mature height of any footing.

## 5. Open points

1. **Which boundary is the Ambo Road**, and is there a usable northern
   frontage? Both primary and secondary entrances currently come off the
   eastern road; a northern frontage would separate them further and is
   preferable.
2. **Road reservation setbacks.** The secondary volume sits 1–2 m off the
   eastern boundary in this layout and is the element most exposed to a
   reservation. Coverage headroom exists to pull it back, but the court
   dimension is what absorbs the move.
3. **Confirm F1** — kindergarten at three levels rather than two. If the
   client wants the kindergarten held to two storeys, the court goes back
   to a roof (F2) and the savings of R2 §3 are lost.
4. Site-specific boreholes, per the Ground and Survey Review query sheet.
