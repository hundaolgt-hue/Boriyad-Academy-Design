Design Brief 01 §10.2 states that "the site geometry does not naturally yield an east–west axis", and Massing Study 01 argued for holding a site grain believed to run 45° off the cardinals. Both were working from a verbal description of a corner plot, and both were wrong. The east boundary is within 1.75° of true north–south.

The most valuable thing in the survey

Building parallel to the boundaries now is building east–west. Every classroom can face north or south, shaded by a 1.2 m horizontal overhang, with no residual triangles and no rotation penalty. The brief called this "the single most valuable move available to the design." It turns out to be free.

The second finding runs the other way. The north and south boundaries are diagonal, so the plot wedges at both ends. The largest true-cardinal rectangle inside the polygon is 51 m east–west by 68 m north–south — the usable depth is 68 m, not the 84.5 m of the bounding extent. The remaining 1,150 m² is a western and southern wedge: good for landscape, drop-off and the agricultural plot, useless for orthogonal building.

02

The ground report is not for this site — and its own data undercut its recommendation

Two separate problems. One is positional and may be a datum error. The other is a technical gap that matters more.

The boreholes sit south of the boundary

The eastings overlap the site. The northings do not — every one of the six holes falls outside the polygon, between 36 and 84 m south of the southern boundary. The report is also addressed to Three M JY Trading Plc and locates the works at "Sheger city, Melka Nano Sub city around Amfomeda", not Ashewa Meda.

A hand-held GPS precision error is ±5–10 m and cannot account for this. Either the report belongs to the adjacent parcel, or the survey and the report sit on different datums — an Adindan/WGS84 mismatch produces offsets of exactly this order in Ethiopia. Either way it cannot carry foundation design here. Four boreholes to 15 m inside the polygon are needed, and the rockhead variability below makes that urgent rather than procedural.

A plasticity index of 63 at a liquid limit of 105 is extreme black cotton. The BH-2 sample at 2.50–3.00 m sits at the report's own recommended founding depth of 3.00 m, and still swells 120%. The report never mentions heave, gives no swell pressure, and does not establish the depth of the active zone — which in Addis Ababa black cotton typically runs 2.5 to 4.0 m.

The report adopts BH-3 as the design case, not the weakest hole, and issues an identical recommendation for G+4 and G+6. Its conclusion that "uniform soil layers are identified" is contradicted by its own logs, which show rockhead between 3.5 m and 15.0 m across roughly 50 m. Carry 275 kPa until site-specific holes say otherwise. Even then the ground is strong — bearing is not what constrains this project.

One further item for the structural engineer: the report assigns importance factor I = 1.0. Under ES EN 1998-1 a school is Importance Class III, γI = 1.2. The design seismic action is 0.12 g, not the 0.10 g stated.

Why this makes the basement nearly free

The design has to reach below the black cotton regardless — that is precisely why the report founds at 3.00 m. A basement slab at about 4.0 m takes the whole structure clear of the active zone, removes the expansive material instead of founding within it, and turns the ground floor into a suspended slab, which expansive soil demands anyway and which would otherwise be a cost with no return. No groundwater was found to 15 m, so there is no dewatering, no tanking against head, and no uplift case. The marginal excavation is roughly one metre beyond what was already required.

The caution: rockhead at 3.5 m in the southern holes. If that trend reaches the site, a 4.0 m basement excavates fractured basalt — rippable with a breaker rather than blasted, but a real cost and programme item. Rockhead deepens northward, to 15 m at the northernmost hole. Put the basement where the new boreholes show the deepest rockhead.

The court is 1,288 m². At 0.5 m² per student in ranks that holds 2,576 — the entire primary school in one assembly, or any single school comfortably, with the staggered arrival the brief already assumes for stair and lift loading. Art. 12 permits it, since full-day operation is retained.

04

Scheme F — three buildings around one court

The instruction that the three sections be separated with different entrances ruled out the previous scheme, which shared plates between the kindergarten and the primary school. One school, one volume, one gate.

Sized independently, the three schools need 2,784 m² of footprint — 60% coverage against a 63% ceiling. Area was never the problem. Depth is. A classroom bar is 17–19 m and a court holding the 32 × 19 m rectangle needs 21 m, so three parallel bars plus a court comes to 72 m against the 68 m of usable cardinal depth, before a single joint or setback.

The tall building is on the right side of the court

At 9° North the sun tracks south of zenith for eight and a half months of the year. A seven-storey volume on the north side of a court throws its shadow away from the court rather than across it — so the primary building can take the full height without darkening the sports and assembly space. The secondary volume on the east shades the court in early morning only; the three-storey kindergarten on the south casts a short shadow, and does so during the kiremt rains when the court is least used.

The one compromise, and where it is placed

Kindergarten and primary run true east–west, so every classroom in both faces north or south behind a 1.2 m overhang. The secondary runs north–south along the road, and its long faces therefore look east and west. That falls on the secondary deliberately:

  • It has the fewest classrooms — 25 against the primary's 40.
  • It carries the area-hungry specialist rooms — three 22 × 9 m laboratories, the 24 × 8 m workshop, the 425.75 m² hall, the 272 m² library. These are indifferent to orientation and take the eastern, road-facing side, where they also buffer traffic noise.
  • Its classrooms face west over the court behind a 3 m veranda with vertical fins. Horizontal shading is useless at 10–30° solar altitude; fins are the correct device, and this veranda is where Brief 01 §4.3 always intended the building's character to sit. It overlooks the sports and the assembly — the best room in the school.
  • Oldest students are the ones best able to use shaded outdoor circulation rather than a sealed corridor.

And each school now owns its own roof terrace outright, which no shared-mass scheme could deliver. The basement — 810 m² under the secondary volume, not the full footprint — takes the bulk water tank, the rainwater cistern, the treatment plant, generator and MV, bulk stores and kitchen support, with its ramp off the south-east forecourt.

What separation buys, having first read it as a cost

Massing Study 01 discarded multi-volume schemes on one number: facade area per square metre of floor, 0.64 against 0.46. That was too narrow a test. Separation turns out to pay for itself several times over.

  • Three independent regular rectangles with movement joints. At 0.12 g this is the configuration you want — no torsional irregularity, no re-entrant corners, shear-wall centroid on the mass centroid in each volume. The eroding courtyard of Study 01, with set-backs spiralling around four faces, was plan-irregular at every level.
  • Art. 15 satisfied without argument. Each school demonstrably maintains its own administration in its own building, which narrows the regulatory ask to the Art. 6 compound-area waiver alone.
  • Evacuation simplifies. Three separate populations, three separate egress systems, no shared stair carrying 3,600 people.
  • Phasing becomes trivial. The kindergarten is a three-storey building that can be tendered, built and occupied on its own. Brief 01 §9.3 wanted exactly this and judged it difficult on a single-core building.
  • Repetitive formwork on one 8.0 m grid, no transfer, no long spans, no post-tensioning.

The honest cost: three sets of cores, lifts, stacks and plant, and more envelope per square metre of floor. But Principle 3 — three schools, three entrances, three cores — was always going to buy the cores.

One assumption in the brief that the schedule does not support

Brief 01 Principle 3 has the three schools sharing "one hall, one library spine and one sports deck." Read against the guideline, only the sports provision is genuinely shared. Art. 8.2(4) schedules two primary libraries separately from the Art. 8.3(10) secondary library, and the Art. 8.3(9) multipurpose hall belongs to the secondary school. Nothing in the schedule actually needed to be shared — which is why full separation costs so much less than it first appears.

Carried forward unchanged

  • The 8.0 m grid. Classroom 8.0 × 7.0 = 56.00 m²; kindergarten 8.0 × 7.875 = 63.00 m². Bars 17 m deep, 7 + 3 + 7.
  • Kindergarten in clusters of five around a shared Golee commons — four clusters, matching the four registrable KG units.
  • Vertically aligned sanitary stacks, roughly 144 WCs, waterborne, with the treatment plant in the basement and treated effluent irrigating the agricultural plot.
  • Deep verandas and perforated screens on the west-facing ends — which the corrected orientation reduces to the short ends of the bars rather than a long elevation.
05

Open points

Confirm the kindergarten at three levels

Option F1, and the whole scheme turns on it. Hold the kindergarten to two storeys and the court goes back onto a roof, taking the deck structure, the waterproofing and the elevated ball-catch enclosure with it.

Which boundary is the Ambo Road — and is there a usable northern frontage?

Both the primary and secondary gates currently come off the eastern road, 55 m apart. A northern frontage would separate them properly and is preferable. The kindergarten sits at the far south-west, the quietest corner, on the assumption that the eastern road is the principal one.

Road reservation setbacks on each frontage

The secondary volume sits 1–2 m off the eastern boundary and is the element most exposed to a reservation. There are eleven percentage points of coverage in hand, but the court dimension is what absorbs any move — and the court is where the sports and the flag ceremony both live.

Reconcile the borehole positions with NATM

Datum, coordinate basis, and whether the report relates to this parcel. Then commission four holes to 15 m inside the polygon, with swell pressure and active-zone depth reported. A full query sheet is in the ground review.

One landscape constraint worth stating early

No high-water-demand trees near the buildings on this soil — eucalyptus in particular must stay beyond about 1.5 times its mature height from any footing, or it will drive seasonal heave and settlement directly. This runs against the guideline's Gaaddisa Dubbisaa, a reading place under trees, and against the Art. 7(5) greenery. Both belong in the court and the western wedge, at distance, or in raised planters.
